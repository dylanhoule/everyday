# Training & Nutrition Plan

**Stats:** 165 lb, 5'8", ~15-17% body fat
**Goals:** Ultramarathon + climber-style upper body (Magnus Midtbø physique)
**Current:** 20 mpw, ramping to 40 mpw

---

## The Core Tension

Running and hypertrophy compete. At 40 mpw you can hold leg muscle but not build it. Upper body is largely spared, which is convenient, because the physique you want is mostly upper body. Midtbø's legs are unremarkable. Lats, traps, shoulders, forearms are the whole look.

**The main rules:**

1. Never cut calories while ramping mileage. Cut now, at 20 mpw, then eat at maintenance as volume climbs.
2. Protein is non-negotiable at 165 g/day.
3. Getting lean is the fast part (3 months). Building back width is the long project (2-3 years).
4. If upper body shrinks, you're underfed, not undertrained. That's the most likely failure mode.

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Running | Lifting |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Off | Pull, upper only |
| **Tue** | Easy 4-5 mi | — |
| **Wed** | Easy 4-5 mi | Legs + grip, PM, 25 min |
| **Thu** | Quality: tempo or hills | — |
| **Fri** | Off | Push, upper only |
| **Sat** | Easy shakeout 3-4 mi | — |
| **Sun** | Long run | — |

**Why it's built this way:** Monday and Friday are running-free, so they hold the heavy upper work. Wednesday is the only sensible leg slot, three days after the long run and four days before the next. Saturday primes Sunday and must stay short.

## Lifting Sessions

### Monday — Pull

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Weighted pull-up | 5 x 4-6 |
| Barbell or chest-supported row | 4 x 8-10 |
| Lat pulldown, wide | 3 x 10-12 |
| Chest-supported rear delt raise | 3 x 15 |
| Barbell or incline curl | 3 x 8-10 |
| Front lever progression | 5 x 10-15s |
| Ab wheel | 3 x 8-12 |

The weighted pull-up is the single highest-value lift in this plan. Work toward bodyweight +30-40% for reps.

### Wednesday — Legs + Grip (25-30 min)

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 x 8 each |
| Single leg Romanian deadlift | 3 x 10 each |
| Standing calf raise | 4 x 12 |
| Tibialis raise | 3 x 20 |
| Copenhagen plank | 3 x 20s each |
| Farmer's carry | 4 x 40m heavy |
| Dead hang, open hand | 4 x 20-30s |

This is injury prevention, not growth. No back squats, no barbell RDLs — heavy bilateral leg work alongside rising mileage buys fatigue and injury risk, not size. Calf and tib work is what keeps Achilles and shin problems away.

### Friday — Push

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Overhead press | 4 x 5-6 |
| Weighted dip | 4 x 6-8 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 x 8-10 |
| Lateral raise | 4 x 12-15 |
| Cable external rotation | 3 x 15 |
| Overhead tricep extension | 3 x 10-12 |
| Hanging leg raise | 3 x max |

---

## Progression

**Weighted pull-up, weighted dip, overhead press:** add 2.5 kg when you hit the top of the rep range across all sets.

**Everything else:** add reps first, then weight.

**Deload every 6-8 weeks:** halve all sets, keep the top weights. Align it with a running down week.

---

## Scaling Lifting With Mileage

| Mileage | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| **Under 30** | Run as written |
| **30-35** | Drop lat pulldown, incline DB press, one curl set |
| **35+** | Drop standing calf raises and Bulgarian split squats. Halve accessory volume on Mon and Fri. Keep weighted pull-up, OHP, weighted dip at full sets. You're maintaining, not building. |

## Nutrition — Current Deficit Phase (20 mpw)

**Weekly average: 2,450 kcal. Protein 165 g every day. Fat floor 60 g.**

| Day | Training | Kcal | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Pull | 2,250 | 165 g | 210 g | 75 g |
| **Tue** | Easy run | 2,400 | 165 g | 260 g | 70 g |
| **Wed** | Easy run + legs | 2,450 | 165 g | 270 g | 70 g |
| **Thu** | Quality run | 2,600 | 165 g | 320 g | 65 g |
| **Fri** | Push | 2,250 | 165 g | 210 g | 75 g |
| **Sat** | Shakeout | 2,450 | 165 g | 290 g | 65 g |
| **Sun** | Long run | 2,750 | 165 g | 350 g | 65 g |

Weekly total ~17,150 kcal, about 350/day below maintenance. Expect 0.5-0.7 lb/week.

**On carb cycling:** the low days are lift days, not rest days. Deliberate. Lifting is far less glycogen-dependent than running, and Mon/Fri have no running at all. The fat-loss benefit of cycling is negligible once calories and protein are matched — what it buys you is performance timing. Worth more past 30 mpw than it is now.

**Don't go aggressive.** Sub-200 g carb days alongside running and lifting cost more in session quality than they return.

### When to stop cutting

10-12 weeks, or 155 lb, whichever comes first. Then move to the maintenance table.

**Exit early if:**
- Weighted pull-up drops for two consecutive weeks
- Resting heart rate climbs 5+ bpm
- Sleep degrades

Those mean the deficit is too deep regardless of what the scale says.

## Nutrition — Maintenance, Scaling With Mileage

Protein and fat stay roughly fixed. Carbs absorb almost all of the increase.

| Mileage | Calories | Carbs | Protein | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 2,700-2,900 | 340-380 g | 150-165 g | 80-90 g |
| 25 | 2,850-3,050 | 380-420 g | 150-165 g | 80-90 g |
| 30 | 3,000-3,200 | 420-460 g | 155-170 g | 85-95 g |
| 35 | 3,150-3,350 | 460-500 g | 155-170 g | 85-95 g |
| 40 | 3,300-3,600 | 500-550 g | 160-175 g | 90-100 g |

**The rule underneath:** ~100 kcal per mile. Every 5 mpw adds ~500 kcal/week, or 70/day. Round up rather than down.

### Daily variation at maintenance

| Day | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Mon, Fri (lift, no run) | Baseline −200 |
| Tue, Wed (easy runs) | Baseline |
| Thu (quality) | Baseline +150 |
| Sat (shakeout) | Baseline +100, front-load carbs |
| Sun (long run) | Baseline +300 to +700 by distance |

## Protein Practicalities

Four feedings of ~40 g, not two of 65 g plus scattered incidentals. Muscle protein synthesis responds per meal and there's a ceiling per feeding.

One feeding within an hour of every lift and every long run. A pre-sleep dose of 40 g casein or Greek yogurt is worth it on lift days.

**Easy 35 g additions if you're short:**
- 250 g cottage cheese: 30 g
- Whey shake + banana: 25 g
- 150 g chicken added to lunch: 35 g

**Sources:** eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken thigh, salmon, lean beef, cottage cheese, whey. Salmon and eggs twice a week each covers most of what runners commonly run low on.

### Sample day (~2,450 kcal, 165 g protein)

| Meal | Food |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | 80 g oats, 40 g whey, banana |
| Lunch | 200 g chicken, 200 g cooked rice, vegetables, olive oil |
| Pre-lift | Greek yogurt + honey + berries |
| Dinner | 200 g salmon or lean beef, potato or pasta, salad |
| Pre-bed | 250 g cottage cheese |

## Long Run Fueling

| Long run | Fueling |
|---|---|
| Under 75 min | Water only |
| 75-120 min | 30-40 g carbs/hr |
| 2-3 hrs | 50-60 g carbs/hr, 500 mg sodium/hr |
| 3 hrs+ | 60-90 g carbs/hr, 700-1,000 mg sodium/hr |

**Before:** 60-100 g carbs, low fiber and low fat, 90 min out. Bagel with honey, or oats with banana and no nuts.

**After:** 60-80 g carbs + 40 g protein within an hour.

**Start practicing in-run fueling now, at 20 mpw, even when you don't strictly need it.** Gut carb absorption is trainable and takes 8-12 weeks. GI failure ends more ultras than legs do. Runners who first test 80 g/hr during race week discover their stomach can't handle it with no time left to fix it.

No fasted long runs.

---

## Micronutrients

**Iron.** High-mileage runners lose iron through foot-strike hemolysis and sweat. Get ferritin tested before supplementing. Below 30 ng/mL and you'll feel it in your runs.

**Sodium.** 500-1,000 mg/hr on long runs in heat. Under-replacing sodium is more common than under-replacing water.

**Vitamin D.** Test it. Affects bone density, which matters at this mileage.

**Calcium.** 1,000+ mg. Stress fracture risk rises sharply with volume, and inadequate energy intake is the main driver.

---

## Supplements Worth Taking

- **Creatine, 5 g daily.** Helps the lifting, no meaningful downside for endurance. The water weight is intramuscular, not bloat.
- **Whey**, for convenience hitting protein.

Everything else is optional.

---

## Monitoring

Weigh three mornings a week, same conditions. Track the **weekly average**, not daily numbers.

- Dropping more than 0.5%/week → add 200 kcal/day
- Climbing more than 0.25%/week while lean → cut 150 kcal/day

The tables are estimates. Your actual expenditure could be 15% off either way. Three weeks of scale data tells you the truth.

---

## Rules That Matter More Than Exercise Selection

**Saturday stays easy and short.** It exists to prime Sunday, not to add mileage. Cut it before you cut anything else.

**Tuesday is the recovery day disguised as a run.** Keep it conversational. It's the day after leg work and people habitually run it too hard.

**If Thursday's quality run degrades two weeks running, cut Wednesday's lift**, not the run. The running progression matters more for finishing an ultra and you'd lose maybe 10% of your upper body gains.

**Never lift legs the day before the long run.** Never lift heavy the day after it.

**No hangboarding for at least six months** if you take up climbing. Finger pulleys adapt on an 18-24 month timeline while muscle adapts in 8-12 weeks. That gap is why so many climbers blow out an A2 pulley in year one. The dead hangs in this plan are sufficient.

---

## Low Energy Availability — The Thing to Watch

The single most common problem in runners who also lift. It doesn't announce itself as hunger.

**Signs:**
- Stalled lifting numbers
- Poor sleep
- Cold hands
- Resting heart rate drifting up
- Drop in libido

Two or more of these and you're underfueling. The fix is food, not more discipline.

---

## Realistic Expectations

Roughly half the upper body progress you'd make without the running. No leg growth at all.

Getting to 12-13% body fat takes about 3 months and will make you look lean and athletic. It won't make you look like Midtbø, because that depends on lat and trap development built over years of elite climbing from childhood. Getting lean is the fast part. The rest is the long project — 18-36 months of consistent work for a visible transformation.

Which is the argument for keeping the deficit modest. You want to be building the whole time.
