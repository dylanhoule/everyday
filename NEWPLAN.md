# Training & Nutrition Plan

**Stats:** 165 lb · 5'8" · 18 years old · ~15-17% body fat
**Primary goal:** Lean, climber-style upper body — lats, traps, delts, forearms, low body fat
**Secondary goal:** 50K ultramarathon, no date set
**Current running:** ~20 mi/week, ~9:00 easy pace

---

## The Decision This Plan Makes

These two goals compete, so this plan runs them **in sequence, not in parallel.**

Running fitness is a fast, repeatable adaptation. From a 22 mi/week base you can build to a 50K in 20 weeks, and you can do that in any year of your life. Lat width, trap thickness and forearm development take 18-36 months, and every week spent above ~35 mi/week runs that development at roughly half speed. You are 18. This is the best muscle-building window you will ever have, and it is the one that doesn't come back.

So the 28-week 50K plan goes on the shelf. Running stays **flat at 22-25 mi/week, permanently.** That is not a holding pattern — it's a good place to live:

- Maintains real aerobic fitness with one threshold session a week, so you keep getting faster
- At that volume, interference with **upper body** hypertrophy is minimal (running competes with legs, not lats — and your goal is almost entirely upper body)
- Leaves a base you can start a 20-week ultra build from on four weeks' notice
- Frees a fourth weekly lifting session, which matters more for the physique than anything else in this document

### Three phases

| Phase | When | Running | Calories | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Cut** | Now → Oct 26, 2026 or 155 lb (11 wks) | 22 mi/wk flat | 2,550 avg | 165 → 158 lb, ~12% BF |
| **2 — Lean gain** | Nov 2026 → 12-18 months | 25 mi/wk flat | 3,150 | Build the width |
| **3 — 50K build** | When you pick a date | 25 → 48 mi/wk | Maintenance only | Race |

**Rule zero: never cut and ramp mileage at the same time.** Phase 1 works because mileage is flat. Phase 3 works because calories are flat. Overlapping them is how 18-year-old runners get stress fractures.

|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Off | **Pull A** — heavy, thickness |
| **Tue** | Easy 4 mi + strides | **Push A** — heavy (PM) |
| **Wed** | Quality: tempo / intervals | — |
| **Thu** | Easy 4 mi | **Legs + grip** — 25 min (PM) |
| **Fri** | Off | **Pull B** — width, volume |
| **Sat** | Easy 4 mi | **Push B** — delts, arms, forearms (PM) |
| **Sun** | Long run 7-9 mi | — |

**Why it's built this way.** Monday and Friday are run-free, so they hold the two heaviest lifts. Wednesday's quality run sits two days after heavy pull and follows only an easy run. Leg work lands Thursday, three clear days before Sunday's long run and one day after the quality session, not before it — this is the fix for the single worst scheduling conflict in the original plans. Upper body lifting on run days is fine; separate them by 6+ hours where you can.

**Pull frequency is 2x/week and push is 2x/week.** That is the change that builds the physique. Three full-body-ish sessions a week was always the compromise forced by 40+ mi/week.

# RUNNING

## The rules

1. **Almost everything is easy.** Conversational, full sentences, nose-breathing. Jogging everything in one gray zone is exactly why 9:00 is your only gear.
2. **One hard session per week.** Wednesday. That's where speed comes from.
3. **Volume stays flat.** 22 mi/week in Phase 1, 25 in Phase 2. Do not creep. If you feel great, run the same mileage faster on Wednesday, not more miles.
4. **Every 4th week is a cutback:** drop to ~17 mi and make Wednesday easy + strides.
5. **Long run cap:** never more than ~10% above your longest run of the past 30 days.

## Pace zones

*Anchored to a ~9:00 easy pace. Run the time trial and reset off real numbers.*

| Zone | RPE | Feel | Est. pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recovery | 3-4 | Feels almost too slow | 9:45 - 10:45 |
| Easy / aerobic | 4-5 | Full sentences, nose-breathing | 8:45 - 9:30 |
| Marathon / ultra effort | 6 | Short sentences only | 8:10 - 8:40 |
| Threshold / tempo | 7-8 | "Comfortably hard," ~1-hr race effort | 7:50 - 8:15 |
| VO2 / 5K | 9 | Labored, sustainable 3-5 min | 7:10 - 7:40 |
| Strides | — | Smooth fast turnover, NOT a sprint | ~6:50 - 7:20 feel |
| Hill sprints | — | 10-20s near-max uphill, full walk-down | by effort |

**From a 1-mile time trial:** threshold ≈ mile pace + 45-60 s/mi · easy ≈ mile pace + 2:00-2:30/mi · VO2 ≈ mile pace + 15-25 s/mi.

**Time trial protocol:** 10 min easy warm-up + a few strides, then run 1 mile as a hard, *even* effort. Rebuild all zones from the result.

**Test schedule:** now (baseline), then every 12 weeks. A 5K time trial or parkrun works too once you're fitter.

## Quality session rotation (Wednesday)

Repeat this 4-week cycle indefinitely. Week 4 is the cutback.

| Week | Session | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cruise intervals** | WU 10 min. 5 x 3 min @ threshold w/ 90s jog. CD 10 min. |
| 2 | **Tempo** | WU 10 min. 20 min continuous @ threshold. CD 10 min. |
| 3 | **Hill sprints** | WU. 8 x 15s steep hill near-max, walk fully down. + 4 strides. CD. |
| 4 | **Cutback** | Easy + 6 x 20s strides. Nothing hard. |

Progress it by adding volume to the interval, not intensity: 5 x 3 min → 6 x 3 min → 3 x 6 min → 2 x 10 min → 25 min continuous. Then restart the cycle at the harder end.

# LIFTING

Full commercial gym assumed. **The weighted pull-up is the single highest-value lift here** — work toward bodyweight +30-40% for reps. It is the lift that builds the look you're after.

| Barbell shrug | 3 x 10-12 |
| Barbell curl | 3 x 8-10 |
| Ab wheel | 3 x 8-12 |

## Tuesday — Push A (heavy)

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Overhead press | 4 x 5-6 |
| Weighted dip | 4 x 6-8 |
| Incline dumbbell press | 3 x 8-10 |
| Lateral raise | 3 x 12-15 |
| Overhead tricep extension | 3 x 10-12 |
| Cable external rotation | 3 x 15 |

## Thursday — Legs + Grip (25-30 min)

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 x 8 each |
| Single-leg Romanian deadlift | 3 x 10 each |

This is durability work, not growth work. No back squats, no barbell deadlifts — heavy bilateral leg work alongside running buys fatigue and injury risk, not size. The calf and tib work is what keeps Achilles and shin problems away, and the carries and hangs are building the forearms.

| Exercise | Sets x Reps |
|---|---|
| Seated dumbbell shoulder press | 3 x 8-10 |
| Cable lateral raise | 4 x 12-15 |
| Machine or cable chest press | 3 x 10-12 |
| Overhead cable tricep extension | 3 x 12-15 |
| Wrist curl + reverse wrist curl | 3 x 15 each |
| Hanging leg raise | 3 x max |

## Progression

**Weighted pull-up, weighted dip, overhead press, barbell row:** add 2.5 kg when you hit the top of the rep range across all sets.

**Everything else:** add reps first, then weight.

**Deload every 6-8 weeks:** halve all sets, keep the top weights. Align it with a running cutback week.

**In Phase 1 (cut), don't chase PRs.** Holding your numbers while losing 7 lb *is* progress. Expect the weighted pull-up to go up anyway, because it's a bodyweight lift and you're getting lighter.

# NUTRITION

## The formula

Forget calorie tables that scale by activity level. Yours is:

> **Maintenance ≈ 2,470 + (14 × weekly miles)**

That's 2,780 at 22 mi/week and 2,820 at 25. It's built from your actual resting rate (~1,740 by both Mifflin-St Jeor and Katch-McArdle), the thermic effect of food, four lifting sessions, and ~95 kcal per mile run. It replaces both tables in the original plans, one of which added calories at double its own stated rate and would have had you 350/day over maintenance at peak mileage.

**Every calorie number below is a starting hypothesis.** Three weeks of scale data overrides it.

## Phase 1 — Cut (now → Oct 26)

**Weekly average 2,550. Protein 165 g every day, no exceptions. Fat floor 60 g.**

| Day | Training | Kcal | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Pull A | 2,575 | 165 g | 310 g | 75 g |
| **Tue** | Easy run + Push A | 2,550 | 165 g | 315 g | 70 g |
| **Wed** | Quality run | 2,650 | 165 g | 350 g | 65 g |
| **Thu** | Easy run + legs | 2,500 | 165 g | 300 g | 70 g |
| **Fri** | Pull B | 2,450 | 165 g | 280 g | 75 g |
| **Sat** | Easy run + Push B | 2,370 | 165 g | 270 g | 70 g |
| **Sun** | Long run | 2,750 | 165 g | 375 g | 65 g |

~230/day below maintenance. The deficit block ends 2026-10-26, or the day the scale reads 155 lb, whichever comes first.

Carbs sit high on Monday and low on Saturday: Monday follows the long run and holds your heaviest pulling session, Saturday is isolation work and an easy 3. Weekly total is unchanged.

**Why the low days are lift days, not rest days:** lifting is far less glycogen-dependent than running, and Mon/Fri have no running at all. The fat-loss benefit of carb cycling is zero once calories and protein match — what it buys is performance timing.

**Don't go deeper than this.** At 2,450 with this training load your energy availability sits at ~32 kcal/kg of lean mass. Below 30 is where bone density, hormones and recovery start failing, and at 18 with rising bone load that's not a slow-progress problem, it's a stress fracture problem. There is no version of this where eating less gets you there faster.

### Exit the cut early if any of these

- Weighted pull-up drops two weeks running
- Resting heart rate climbs 5+ bpm
- Sleep degrades
- Cold hands, drop in libido, persistent low mood

Two or more and you're underfueling. The fix is food, not more discipline. This is the most common failure mode for people who run and lift at the same time, and it does not announce itself as hunger.

## Long run fueling

At 7-9 mile long runs you don't strictly need in-run fuel. **Practice it anyway.** Gut carb absorption is trainable and takes 8-12 weeks to develop. GI failure ends more ultras than legs do, and runners who first test 80 g/hr during race week discover their stomach can't handle it with no time left to fix it.

| Duration | Fueling |
|---|---|
| Under 75 min | Water only |
| 75-120 min | 30-40 g carbs/hr |
| 2-3 hrs | 50-60 g carbs/hr, 500 mg sodium/hr |
| 3 hrs+ | 60-90 g carbs/hr, 700-1,000 mg sodium/hr |

**Before:** 60-100 g carbs, low fiber and low fat, 90 min out. Bagel with honey, or oats with banana and no nuts.
**After:** 60-80 g carbs + 40 g protein within an hour.

**No fasted long runs.** Ever, in either phase.

## Micronutrients

**Iron.** Runners lose iron through foot-strike hemolysis and sweat. Don't supplement iron blind: iron overload is worse than deficiency.

**Vitamin D.** Test it. Drives bone density, which is the thing at risk here.

**Calcium.** 1,000+ mg daily. Stress fracture risk rises with running volume, and inadequate energy intake is the main driver.

**Sodium.** 500-1,000 mg/hr on long runs in heat. Under-replacing sodium is more common than under-replacing water.

## Supplements worth taking

- **Creatine, 5 g daily.** Helps the lifting, no meaningful downside for endurance. The water weight is intramuscular, not bloat.
- **Whey**, for convenience hitting protein.

Everything else is optional.

# MONITORING

Weigh **three mornings a week**, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food or water. Track the **weekly average**, never a single day.

| Phase | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | Losing >0.8 lb/week | Add 200 kcal/day |
| Cut | Losing <0.25 lb/week over 3 wks | Cut 150 kcal/day |
| Lean gain | Gaining >0.5 lb/week | Cut 150 kcal/day |
| Lean gain | Flat for 3 wks | Add 200 kcal/day |

**Also track, every week:** weighted pull-up top set, overhead press top set, and a photo in the same light and pose. The scale alone will mislead you in Phase 2 — the photos and the pull-up number are the real progress markers.

Three weeks of data beats any calculator, including this one. Your actual expenditure could be 15% off in either direction.

# RULES THAT MATTER MORE THAN EXERCISE SELECTION

**If Wednesday's quality run degrades two weeks running, cut Thursday's leg session** — not the run. Legs aren't growing at this mileage anyway.

**Never lift legs the day before the long run. Never lift heavy the day after it.**

**Tuesday and Saturday easy runs are recovery disguised as training.** Keep them conversational. People habitually run these too hard, which turns a polarized week into a gray-zone week and stalls everything.

**Volume creep is the enemy.** Adding "just a couple miles" because you feel good is how 22 mi/week becomes 32 mi/week becomes a plan that no longer builds anything. The mileage cap is a feature.

**No hangboarding for at least six months** if you take up climbing. Finger pulleys adapt on an 18-24 month timeline while muscle adapts in 8-12 weeks. That gap is why so many climbers rupture an A2 pulley in year one. The dead hangs here are sufficient.

**On climbing generally:** it's the fastest route to the specific look you want, and it's worth adding in Phase 2 — but as a *replacement* for Friday's Pull B, not on top of it. Two sessions a week, bouldering, no hangboard. Adding it as a fifth training day on top of running and four lifts is how you end up injured.

# WHEN YOU PICK A RACE DATE

Take the 28-week 50K plan and slot in at **Week 9**, not Week 1. You'll already be at its Week 9 mileage (26 mi), which turns it into a **20-week build.** Count backward 20 weeks from race day.

Three things to change when you do:

1. **Stop the lean gain and eat at maintenance for the whole build.** Use the same formula: 2,470 + 14 per weekly mile. That's 3,120 at the 48 mi/week peak. Never cut while ramping.
2. **Drop to two lifts a week from the plan's Week 17 onward** (38+ mi/week): Monday Pull A and Tuesday Push A only, at ~60% of listed volume. Keep the weighted pull-up, row, OHP and dip at full sets — everything else goes. Move calf raises, tib raises and Copenhagen planks onto Monday as a 10-minute block. You're maintaining, not building, and that's the correct trade for 20 weeks.
3. **Add a race-week carb load,** which the original plan is missing entirely. Two days out, 8-10 g of carbs per kg bodyweight — around 600-700 g/day for you. That is an uncomfortable amount of food and it needs rehearsing before a long run in training, not discovered the night before the race.

Also watch the combined weekend load in that plan. It goes from 10 miles to 32 miles (12 Sat + 20 Sun) by Week 22. Each individual jump obeys the 10% rule but the aggregate is aggressive. If something breaks, it'll be there.

# REALISTIC EXPECTATIONS

**Phase 1, by early November:** 158 lb at roughly 12% body fat. You will look meaningfully leaner and more defined, abs visible, and this is the fastest visible change you'll get. It is almost entirely a fat-loss result, not a muscle-building one.

**Phase 2, twelve months in:** somewhere around 170-175 lb at 12-14%, with genuinely wider lats and thicker traps. That's ~12-15 lb of actual muscle, which is a good year at your training age, and slightly slower than it'd be with zero running.

**On the Midtbø comparison.** What makes that build read the way it does is V-taper from lat width plus low body fat, not overall mass. He's taller than you, so his weight isn't your target — for your frame the equivalent is roughly 170-175 lb at under 10% body fat. That's a 2-3 year project, and it's built almost entirely by weighted pull-ups, heavy rows and being lean. The plan is pointed at exactly those things.

The other half of it is climbing-specific: forearm, finger and grip development from many years on the wall starting young. You can approximate a lot of it with carries, hangs and direct forearm work, and you can close most of the rest by actually climbing in Phase 2.

Getting lean is the fast part. The width is the long project.
